Example Job 1 – Leadership Conference Audio Support

Overview of the Job

I was asked to provide sound support for a leadership conference. The client required a microphone for the guest speakers, as well as audio outputs to both a camera for recording and the venue’s built-in speaker system. The setup needed to be simple and reliable so that the event could run smoothly without distracting from the presentations.

Kit List

  • 20m XLR cable
  • 5m XLR (female) to 1/4 inch (male) cables ×4
  • XLR gender changer
  • 1/4 inch to 3.5mm adapters ×2
  • Wireless Shure SM58 microphone
  • Behringer UMC22 audio interface

How I Set Up the Job

I positioned the audio interface at the back of the room so that I could monitor and adjust levels while remaining out of the way of the audience and speakers.

From the interface, I ran a 20m XLR cable along the side of the room to the front where the venue’s built-in speaker input was located. This was connected using an XLR to 1/4 inch cable and a 3.5mm adapter to match the venue’s input connection.

For the camera recording feed, I used two 5m XLR to 1/4 inch cables from the interface outputs. These were adapted using an XLR gender changer and converted to 3.5mm to match the camera’s audio input.

The microphone input was a wireless Shure SM58, allowing the speaker to move freely without needing to manage a cable.

Difficulties

One of the main challenges was balancing the microphone levels with the venue’s built-in speaker system, as these were not my own front-of-house speakers. This made it difficult to achieve the ideal sound level without feedback or clipping.

I resolved this by carefully adjusting gain levels and helping the speakers understand the correct microphone technique, such as maintaining a consistent distance from the microphone.

Another difficulty was that some speakers were reluctant to use a handheld microphone while presenting. This could have been solved by using a lavalier microphone; however, my lavalier system was broken at the time and I was unable to source a replacement before the event.

Example Job 2 – Spring Showcase

Overview of the Job

I was approached by a music teacher who was organising a 'Spring Showcase' to display the talent within the school and raise money for the music department. I was asked to handle all of the technical aspects of the production, which I was pleased to take on. This was the biggest job I had undertaken to date, involving more than five acts and my first proper use of a digital mixer in a live event setting.

Kit List

  • PreSonus StudioLive 32SC
  • PreSonus StudioLive 18.8 stage box
  • XLR cables
  • Ethernet cable
  • A wide range of microphones

How I Set Up the Job

A week before the showcase, I spent time becoming familiar with the mixer, as I had only used it once before during the previous year’s production. This allowed me to improve my understanding of how a digital mixer operates and become more confident using it.

I began routing audio to the correct outputs, naming channels, and setting up mixes for monitors, lighting, and recording. The day before the show, I brought in the mixer and started moving larger equipment such as amplifiers, the piano, and the drum kit.

With the remaining time, I set up the audio network to the stage box and routed output cables to the lighting box, which gave me control over the PA system. On the day of the show, the majority of the setup was completed. I positioned and connected microphones for the drum kit, amplifiers, choir, and other performers, and ensured all mic stands and cables were ready.

Once this was complete, I worked with the drummer to check microphone placement so that it did not interfere with performance. I then carried out sound checks for the different acts and saved each setup as a scene on the mixer so that I could quickly recall them at the correct point during the show.

Difficulties

The first and only full practice run-through was poorly prepared and unorganised, and it did not go well. However, this became an important learning point, as it highlighted the areas that needed immediate improvement.

By identifying these issues early, I was able to make changes during and after the rehearsal. This helped ensure that the evening performance ran much more smoothly in front of the public.
